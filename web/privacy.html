Privacy policy

Your DNA never leaves your device.

Plain language, true to the code. Updated 2026.

The short version

Three things worth knowing.

  • Your DNA file stays on your device

    Every page that reads a genetic file (23andMe, AncestryDNA, FASTA, FASTQ) processes it in your browser. Nothing uploaded.

  • No accounts, no genetic database

    We never ask who you are and store nothing about your DNA. No server could hold a copy.

  • Open source, so you can check

    The code is public. Open your Network tab: no request carries your file.

What runs on your device

Our web tools are static pages plus JavaScript. Your file is read locally with the browser's FileReader and analyzed on your machine. Pages fetch only their own assets: code, reference data (polygenic scores, ancestry markers), fonts and styles. Your genotypes are never in any request.

Analytics

Pages where you upload a genetic file use cookieless analytics (Cloudflare Web Analytics): one anonymous page view, no cookies, no fingerprinting, nothing about your DNA. Marketing pages like the homepage also use Google Analytics for aggregate traffic, and never read genetic files.

What we do not collect

  • Your DNA, genotypes, or sequence files. They stay in your browser.
  • Names, emails, or accounts. None to create.
  • Your results. Generated locally, saved by you.

Third-party services

Your browser fetches fonts (Google Fonts), icons and libraries from public CDNs, and live star counts from the GitHub API. These providers see standard request data like your IP, as on any site. None receive your genetic data, because it never leaves your browser.

Self-hosting

You can also run DNAnalyzer as a local server or command line tool. See Self-host and Docs. Then all processing happens on infrastructure you control.

A note on genetic data

Genes are permanent. Passwords are not.

Leaked genetic data cannot be changed. That is why DNAnalyzer computes on your device, not the cloud. Nothing here is a medical test; results are educational. For health questions, see a clinician or genetic counselor.

Changes and contact

If this changes, we update this page and the date above. Questions? Open an issue on GitHub or Discord.
